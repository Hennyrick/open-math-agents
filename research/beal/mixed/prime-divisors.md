# A restriction on the cubic base when the fifth-power base is divisible by 5

Let $A,B,C$ be pairwise coprime positive integers satisfying $A^5+B^p=C^3$, with $5\mid A$. For a prime $p>109$ congruent to 3 modulo 4, the following is a consequence of the five inputs to Theorem 6.15 of Jaswal's preprint.
$$
C\equiv\pm1,\ \pm4,\ \pm11\pmod{25}.
$$
The same implication holds for smaller primes outside the exceptional set in that preprint. The five inputs are (LL), (EL), (T2.1), (P7.4)(a) at 3, and (Ex₀), as defined in §2.5. The restriction sharpens the entries for $5\mid X$ in Table 7 for these exponents. It leaves six classes. [Jaswal, §§2.5, 6.4, 6.7 and Table 7](https://manvirjaswal.github.io/papers/x5-y3-zp/jaswal-x5y3zp.pdf)

**The CM field.** Write $X=A$, $Y=-C$, $Z=-B$, so $X^5+Y^3=Z^p$. Let $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)$. Proposition 6.4 of Jaswal excludes a reducible residual Frey representation, since reducibility would imply $30\mid Z$. Lemma 6.7 gives a congruence to a special parameter or a CM form. Theorem 1(a) excludes the special parameters when $5\mid X$. Of the two possible CM fields, $K(\sqrt{-3})$ would force $5\mid Y$ by Proposition 6.6(b), contrary to primitivity. The representation therefore comes from a character of $M=\mathbf Q(\zeta_5)$ by induction to $G_K$. Proposition 6.6(a) gives $6\mid X$. In particular $\gcd(Z,30)=1$. Jaswal explains in §6.4 how (Ex₀) and (P7.4)(a) at 3 give the portion of (Ex) used here. [Jaswal](https://manvirjaswal.github.io/papers/x5-y3-zp/jaswal-x5y3zp.pdf)

**A prime divisor of the variable base.** If $Y$ is a quadratic nonresidue modulo 5, then $Z$ is too, by the equation modulo 5 and oddness of $p$. The sign of $Z$ does not affect this character. The integer $|Z|$ has a prime factor $\ell\equiv2,3\pmod5$. Suppose first that $\ell\ne p$. This prime has residue degree two in $K$, and the prime of $K$ above it is inert in $M/K$. The induced trace is zero there. Formula (40) of Pacetti and Villagra Torcomian gives trace $\pm(\ell^2+1)$ modulo a prime above $p$, since $\ell\mid Z$ and the absolute norm is $\ell^2$. Hence $\ell^2\equiv-1\pmod p$, impossible for $p\equiv3\pmod4$. If $p\equiv11,19\pmod{20}$, every such $\ell$ already differs from $p$. [Pacetti and Villagra Torcomian, §7.4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17845v1)

Formula (40) here also follows from multiplicative reduction in (T2.1). The torus has rank two and its rational character lattice has dimension one over $K$. Frobenius acts through a finite subgroup of $K^\times$, hence by $1$ or $-1$. The toric filtration and cyclotomic determinant give trace $\pm(N\lambda+1)$.

**The place above $p$.** The other two progressions have $p\equiv3,7\pmod{20}$, so $p$ is inert in $K$ and its prime $v$ stays inert in the unramified quadratic extension $M_w/K_v$. If $p\mid Z$, (T2.1) gives multiplicative reduction at $v$. Over the maximal unramified extension, toric uniformization gives two trivial and two cyclotomic composition factors of inertia on the full $p$-torsion. The rank-two constituent has cyclotomic determinant, so its factors are $1,\bar\chi_p$. These characters are distinct because $K_v/\mathbf Q_p$ is unramified and $p>5$. For a representation induced from $G_{M_w}$, the two inertia characters are conjugate by a Frobenius lift. If either one is trivial, both are trivial. Thus $p\nmid Z$ in these progressions too. A prime $\ell\ne p$ as above would again give the impossible square root. [Jaswal, determinant following Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.8](https://manvirjaswal.github.io/papers/x5-y3-zp/jaswal-x5y3zp.pdf)

Consequently $C$ is a quadratic residue modulo 5. Jaswal's Theorem 6.15 already excludes $C\equiv\pm6,\pm8,\pm9,\pm12\pmod{25}$. Removing those possibilities from the quadratic residues leaves $1,4,11,14,21,24$ modulo 25. The variable-prime argument removes $\pm2,\pm3,\pm7$ from the classes retained in Table 7 for $5\mid X$ and these exponents.
